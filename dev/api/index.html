Food webs

FoodWebDynamics.FoodWeb — Type
FoodWeb(A::AbstractMatrix{Bool})

A directed feeding network on S = size(A, 1) species, with A[i, j] == true when species i eats species j (rows are consumers, columns are resources). Basal species (producers) are those with no resources. Construct one directly or with niche_model.

source
FoodWebDynamics.niche_model — Function
niche_model([rng], S, C)

Draw a food web of S species from the niche model of Williams & Martinez (2000) with expected directed connectance C (0 < C < 1/2). The species with the smallest niche value has a feeding range of zero, so the web has at least one basal species. Species are indexed in increasing order of niche value. The model does not depend on this order, but the published median metrics do (see published_pulse_covariance in EcologicalResilience.jl). No webs are rejected here; trophic_levels returns nothing for webs that a community-assembly step should reject.

FoodWebDynamics.trophic_levels — Function
trophic_levels(web::FoodWeb; alive = trues(richness(web)))

Prey-averaged trophic levels among the alive species: 1 for a basal species, and one plus the mean trophic level of its living resources for a consumer. Returns nothing when they are undefined, i.e. when some living consumer has no path to a basal species (for example a closed loop of consumers). Dead species get NaN.

The bioenergetic model

FoodWebDynamics.BioenergeticParameters — Type
BioenergeticParameters(; kwargs...)

Constants of the bioenergetic model of Domínguez-García et al. (2019, SI §8). Keywords and defaults:

  • q = 0.3: Hill coefficient (the functional response has exponent 1 + q);
  • Z = 1.5: predator–prey body-mass ratio, mᵢ = Z^(Lᵢ − 1);
  • K = 1.0: plant carrying capacity; plant growth rate is 1;
  • e_plant = 0.45, e_animal = 0.85: assimilation efficiency on plant and animal prey;
  • x_plant = 0.138, x_animal = 0.314: metabolic constants, xᵢ = x mᵢ^metabolic_exponent;
  • metabolic_exponent = -0.25: printed as +0.25 in the SI;
  • d0 = 0.1: natural mortality as a fraction of metabolism, dᵢ = d0 xᵢ;
  • a0 = 50.0, a0_plant = 10.0: capture coefficients for animal and plant prey;
  • h0 = 0.4: handling-time constant (not given in the paper);
  • alpha_consumer = 0.7, alpha_resource = 0.4, eta_consumer = -0.6, eta_resource = 0.5: means of the per-community allometric exponents of capture (α) and handling (η);
  • exponent_cv = 0.1: their standard deviation as a fraction of the absolute mean;
  • extinction_threshold = 1e-6: a species is extinct below extinction_threshold * mᵢ;
  • plant_metabolism = false: whether plants pay the metabolic loss xᵢBᵢ. SI §8 lists a plant metabolic rate, but in Brose et al. (2006) producers have none, and only false reproduces Dataset S1's total biomass per plant (median 0.98). Natural mortality d0 x mᵢ^metabolic_exponent applies to plants either way.
  • renormalise_diet = false: whether a consumer's diet weight wᵢ is reset to 1/(number of living resources) when a resource dies. By default it stays at 1/(number of resources in the initial web). SI §9 only deletes the dead species' links. Keeping wᵢ reproduces Dataset S1's tolerance to extinctions, cascading extinctions and mortality tolerance at large richness (median TE 0.332 against 0.334 published at N 61–99; renormalising gives 0.393).

The defaults of metabolic_exponent, h0 and plant_metabolism were chosen by matching summaries of Dataset S1 (lab/wiki/model/calibration.md).

FoodWebDynamics.AllometricExponents — Type
AllometricExponents(alpha_consumer, alpha_resource, eta_consumer, eta_resource)

The exponents of one community: capture aᵢⱼ ∝ mᵢ^alpha_consumer mⱼ^alpha_resource and handling hᵢⱼ ∝ mᵢ^eta_consumer mⱼ^eta_resource. The dataset of Domínguez-García et al. (2019) records them per community as a_i, a_j, h_i and h_j.

FoodWebDynamics.FoodWebModel — Type
FoodWebModel(web::FoodWeb, p::BioenergeticParameters, ex::AllometricExponents; mass = nothing)

The bioenergetic model on web, with rates from p and ex. Throws an ArgumentError if the trophic levels of web are undefined (see trophic_levels).

Body masses are Z^(L − 1) from prey-averaged trophic levels L (SI §9), unless mass gives them, e.g. measured masses for an observed web. Measured masses must be relative to the producers' mass, because producers have mass 1 in the model; producers' entries of mass are ignored.

The model is mutable state: alive marks surviving species, mortality holds a multiplier of each species' natural mortality (all ones at baseline) and w the diet weights, 1/(number of resources) (living resources only if params.renormalise_diet). Change them with kill! and set_mortality!. Use rhs! and jacobian! for the vector field and its Jacobian.

source

FoodWebDynamics.reaction_network — Function
reaction_network(model::FoodWebModel, B = nothing; name = :foodweb)

The living part of model as a Catalyst ReactionSystem (requires using Catalyst). There is one species Bᵢ per living population, and the reactions are:

  • ∅ → Bᵢ for plant growth;
  • Bᵢ → ∅ for metabolic and natural losses, with a symbolic mortality multiplier μᵢ;
  • Bⱼ → eⱼ B_c for consumer c feeding on j, with the full functional response as its rate, so the assimilation efficiency is a stoichiometry.

q and K are symbolic too. Returns (network, species, mortality, u0, parameters), where u0 maps species to B (if given) and parameters maps parameters to the model's values. Its ODEs are those of rhs!, and its symbolic Jacobian equals jacobian!.

Assembly and perturbation

FoodWebDynamics.SteadyStateOptions — Type
SteadyStateOptions(; window = 100.0, tolerance = 5e-4, tmax = 1e6, abstol = 1e-10,
                     reltol = 1e-8, polish = true)

How settle! decides that a community is at steady state. The integration proceeds in windows of length window; it stops when every living species' log biomass changed by less than tolerance over the last window (SI §9 uses 0.0005 and leaves the window unstated), or fails at tmax (TSAFE = 10⁶ in SI §9). With polish = true the state is then refined to the equilibrium by Newton's method. Also with polish = true, once the change over a window is below `earlypolish * tolerance, a Newton step is tried, and accepted if it lands on a locally stable equilibrium withinearlypolishdistance(in log biomass) of the current state; this avoids integrating along slow modes for very long times.algis any SciML ODE solver; implicit solvers are given the model's analytic Jacobian.maxitersbounds the number of solver steps of one integration. A run that has not converged stops once no species has gone extinct forstall` time units (default: never); it is then reported as not converged, with its current state (a point on its trajectory, so that further perturbations can continue from it).

source
FoodWebDynamics.settle! — Function
settle!(model::FoodWebModel, B0; options = SteadyStateOptions())

Integrate model from biomasses B0 until steady state, killing species that fall below their extinction threshold (which updates model.alive). Returns (B, converged), where B is the full biomass vector (zero for dead species) and converged is false if options.tmax was reached first, the integration failed, or the Newton polish did not land on a locally stable equilibrium with every survivor above its threshold.

source
FoodWebDynamics.polish! — Function
polish!(model::FoodWebModel, B; maxiter = 50, tol = 1e-11)

Refine B in place to an equilibrium of the living species by Newton's method. Returns true if it converged to a point where every living species is above its extinction threshold and the Jacobian is locally stable (every eigenvalue has negative real part).

FoodWebDynamics.assemble_community — Function
assemble_community([rng], S; params = BioenergeticParameters(), C = 0.15,
                   survival = 0.9, initial_biomass = (0.05, 1.0),
                   options = SteadyStateOptions())

One attempt at the assembly procedure of SI §9: draw a niche web of S species with connectance C and the community's allometric exponents, start from biomasses uniform on initial_biomass, integrate to steady state and remove plants that no living species eats. Returns (community, status). community is nothing and status says why when the attempt is discarded:

  • :trophic_levels: undefined trophic levels (SI: "the network is discarded");
  • :not_converged: no steady state by T_SAFE, or no locally stable equilibrium;
  • :extinctions: fewer than floor(survival * S) species are left once isolated plants are removed;
  • :empty: every survivor was an isolated plant, so nothing is left once they are removed (possible in small webs without consumers).
  • :neutral: the equilibrium has an eigenvalue 0 (to within 10⁻⁹), as when two dynamically identical consumers survive. It is not asymptotically stable, and polish!'s strict < 0 test admits it about half the time because the zero is computed as ±10⁻¹⁷. The published Dataset S1 has none (its smallest Rinf is 1.6×10⁻⁴).

The 90% rule is applied as SI §9 orders it, after isolated plants are removed, to the integer floor(survival * S). Every one of the 19,816 communities of Dataset S1 satisfies N ≥ floor(0.9 iniN) and 5,679 sit exactly at it. The real-valued N ≥ 0.9 iniN would reject those. Applying the rule before the removal would let about 4% of communities fall below the floor; none of the published ones does.

source
FoodWebDynamics.PERTURBATION_STEADY — Constant
PERTURBATION_STEADY

The steady-state settings used after a perturbation. Integration is limited to tmax = 10⁴ (assembly uses T_SAFE = 10⁶). It stops once no species has died for 1000 time units: such a community is oscillating, and its state at that time is used. SI §10 does not say how non-stationary perturbed states were treated.

source
FoodWebDynamics.read_gateway — Function
read_gateway(path; detritus = :basal)

The food webs of GATEWAy v1.0 from its link table (283_2_FoodWebDataBase_2018_12_10.csv). Names are stripped of surrounding spaces and exact duplicate rows dropped. A node's mass is the geometric mean of the masses recorded for it (masses are per link and life stage; −999 means missing). Non-living resources (metabolic type detritus, dead organic material or other) are kept as basal nodes (detritus = :basal) or removed with their links (:drop). Returns a vector of ObservedWeb.

source
FoodWebDynamics.trophic_species — Function
trophic_species(w::ObservedWeb)

Aggregate taxa with identical prey and identical predators (and the same living status) into trophic species, the unit with which Williams & Martinez (2000) compared the niche model with observed webs. Repeat until no two nodes share both sets (merging can make further nodes identical). A trophic species' name joins its taxa with " | ", its mass is the geometric mean of its taxa's recorded masses, and its metabolic type is that of its first taxon.

In the bioenergetic model, taxa with the same links and trophic level get identical parameters. If two of them survive, the equilibrium is neutral (the Jacobian has an eigenvalue 0), so observed webs must be aggregated before computing the stability metrics.

source
FoodWebDynamics.relative_masses — Function
relative_masses(w::ObservedWeb; Z = 1.5)

Consumer masses relative to the producers, for FoodWebModel(...; mass): divided by the geometric mean mass of the basal living nodes with a recorded mass, or, if none has one, by the smallest consumer mass divided by Z (so the lightest consumer sits one niche-model trophic step above the producers). Returns nothing if some consumer has no mass.

Allometric trophic networks

The scaled, unscaled and nutrient models of the ATN family in one general form. Their vector fields and trajectories agree with ATNr 1.1.2; see test/test_atnr.jl.

ATNModel(web, mass, growth; metabolism, rate, numerator, denominator, half_saturation,
         hill, efficiency, interference = 0, base_mortality = 0, threshold = 1e-6)

An allometric trophic network in the general form above. The state is the vector of species biomasses, followed by the nutrient concentrations for NutrientGrowth. Usually built with scaled_atn, unscaled_atn, nutrient_atn or from a FoodWebModel. Mortality multipliers (mortality, all one at baseline) scale base_mortality for press experiments.

LogisticGrowth(r, K, α)

Logistic plant growth rᵢ (1 − Σⱼ αᵢⱼ Bⱼ / Kᵢ) Bᵢ over the living basal species: α = I gives each plant its own resource, α of ones a shared resource pool.

FoodWebDynamics.NutrientGrowth — Type
NutrientGrowth(r, K, v, supply, turnover)

Nutrient-limited plant growth (Schneider et al. 2016): rᵢ Gᵢ Bᵢ with Gᵢ = minₗ Nₗ / (Kₗᵢ + Nₗ), and nutrient pools dNₗ/dt = D (Sₗ − Nₗ) − Σᵢ vₗᵢ rᵢ Gᵢ Bᵢ (D the turnover, S the supply). K and v are nutrients × species.

FoodWebDynamics.scaled_atn — Function
scaled_atn(web; mass, K = 10, x_ratio = 0.314, y = 8, B0 = 0.5, hill = 1.2, interference = 0.8,
           e_plant = 0.545, e_animal = 0.906, competition = :independent, plant_metabolism = true,
           threshold = 1e-6)

The scaled ATN (Delmas et al. 2017; BioEnergeticFoodWebs). Rates are expressed relative to the growth rate of the smallest plant: rᵢ = (mᵢ/m₁)^−0.25 for plants and xᵢ = x_ratio (mᵢ/m₁)^−0.25, with the maximum consumption y relative to metabolism. The functional response is F = w B^h / (B₀^h + c B_c + Σ w B^h) with diet weights w = 1/(number of resources). competition = :shared puts all plants on one resource pool (α of ones).

The defaults are those ATNr 1.1.2 actually initialises (initialise_default_Scaled): x/r = 0.314, y = 8, K = 10, B₀ = 0.5, Hill exponent 1.2, interference 0.8, efficiencies 0.545 (plant food) and 0.906 (animal food), and plants paying metabolism. The test suite checks the vector field against ATNr's.

source
FoodWebDynamics.unscaled_atn — Function
unscaled_atn(web; mass, T = 293.15, k0 = 40, hill = 1.2, interference = 0.8,
             e_plant = 0.545, e_animal = 0.906, competition = :independent, threshold = 1e-6)

The unscaled, temperature-dependent ATN (Binzer et al. 2016), with rates per second and Arrhenius factors (arrhenius). For consumer c and resource r:

  • attack rate a = e^−13.1 m_r^0.25 m_c^−0.8 A(−0.38);
  • handling time h = e^9.66 m_r^−0.45 m_c^0.47 A(0.26);
  • carrying capacity K = k0 m^0.28 A(0.71);
  • plant growth r = e^−15.68 m^−0.25 A(−0.84);
  • metabolism X = e^−16.54 m^−0.31 A(−0.69) for every species.

The functional response is F = a B^h / (1 + c B_c + Σ a h B^h) / m_c. The values are those ATNr 1.1.2 initialises (initialise_default_Unscaled), including k0 = 40, a Hill exponent of 1.2 and efficiencies 0.545 and 0.906. The test suite checks the vector field and trajectories against ATNr's.

FoodWebDynamics.nutrient_atn — Function
nutrient_atn(rng, web; mass, nutrients = 2, T = 293.15, Ropt = 100, hill = 1.2, turnover = 0.25,
             e_plant = 0.545, e_animal = 0.906, threshold = 1e-6)

The ATN with explicit nutrient dynamics (Schneider et al. 2016), with random parameters drawn as in ATNr's default parametrisation:

  • Capture coefficient: b = 50 m_r^{b₁} m_c^{b₂} A(−0.38) L, with b₁ ~ N(0.15, 0.03), b₂ ~ N(0.47, 0.04) and the link strength L (link_strength).
  • Handling time: h = 0.4 m_r^{h₁} m_c^{h₂} A(0.26), with h₁ ~ N(−0.66, 0.02) and h₂ ~ N(−0.48, 0.03).
  • Interference: c ~ N(0.8, 0.2) per consumer, truncated at 0.
  • Plant growth: r = m^−0.25 A(−0.84).
  • Metabolism: X = x₀ m^−0.25 A(−0.69), with x₀ = 0.138 for plants and 0.314 for animals.
  • Nutrients: half-saturation Kₗᵢ ~ U(0.1, 0.2), plant content vₗᵢ ~ U(1, 2) normalised over nutrients, supply Sₗ ~ N(10, 2), turnover D = 0.25.

The functional response is F = w b B^h / (1 + c B_c + Σ w h b B^h) / m_c. The allometric exponents are drawn once per community here. ATNr's description does not say whether they are drawn per community or per link. R_opt = 100 and the L-matrix's γ = 2 are ATNr's defaults.

source

FoodWebDynamics.link_strength — Function
link_strength(m_consumer, m_resource; Ropt = 100, γ = 2)

The allometric link strength L = ((m_c / (m_r R_opt)) e^{1 − m_c/(m_r R_opt)})^γ: one at the optimal consumer–resource mass ratio R_opt, falling off on either side (Schneider et al. 2016). The defaults are those of ATNr's create_Lmatrix, against which the tests check it.
